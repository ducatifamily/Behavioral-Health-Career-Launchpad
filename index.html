Why this work matters

Behavioral health careers are in demand.

WREDI was created to help strengthen the behavioral health workforce in Greater Houston by increasing awareness, experience, education, and pathways into the field.

99% of Texas counties are federally designated Mental Health Professional Shortage Areas.
1 : 980 mental health provider-to-resident ratio reported for Harris, Fort Bend, and Montgomery counties.
Many paths from entry-level support roles to licensed clinical and advanced professional careers.

Start anywhere

There is more than one way into behavioral health.

The careers highlighted below are not the only careers in behavioral health. They represent several high-demand pathways in our region. For some people, an entry-level or certification pathway becomes a lifelong career. For others, it is a way to learn what client-facing work is like before investing in a degree or advanced licensure.

Career pathways

Explore seven directions.

Open a pathway to learn what the work emphasizes, who may enjoy it, and what to explore next.

CHWCommunity Health WorkerOutreach • education • referrals • advocacy • navigation+

Community Health Workers help individuals and families understand health information, locate services, reduce barriers, connect with resources, and communicate with healthcare and social-service organizations.

You may enjoy this if: You like connecting people to practical help and building trust in communities.

Explore the CHW career lesson ↗ Texas CHW certification ↗
MHMental Health Peer SpecialistRecovery • hope • strengths • advocacy • peer support+

Mental Health Peer Specialists use qualifying lived experience with mental health recovery in a trained and professional way to support others, encourage self-advocacy, connect people with resources, and reinforce hope.

You may enjoy this if: You value recovery-oriented support and helping others recognize their own strengths and choices.

Explore the peer specialist career lesson ↗ Texas Certification Board ↗
RSRecovery Support Peer SpecialistSubstance-use recovery • lived experience • recovery planning+

Recovery Support Peer Specialists use qualifying lived experience with substance-use recovery to support people working toward recovery and wellness while emphasizing hope, personal choice, and multiple pathways to recovery.

You may enjoy this if: You want to use recovery experience in a professional support role.

Explore the recovery support career lesson ↗ Texas Certification Board ↗
LCDCSubstance Use Counselor — LCDCAssessment • counseling • treatment planning • documentation+

LCDC work is a clinical substance-use counseling pathway that may include intake, assessment, treatment planning, counseling, education, documentation, and care coordination.

You may enjoy this if: You are drawn to structured clinical counseling, treatment planning, and professional documentation.

Explore the LCDC career lesson ↗ Texas LCDC program ↗
PTPsychiatric TechnicianDirect care • observation • safety • documentation • teamwork+

Psychiatric Technicians provide hands-on support to people receiving mental health, developmental-disability, substance-use, crisis, residential, or hospital-based services.

You may enjoy this if: You prefer direct care, structured environments, observation, safety, and team-based work.

Explore the psychiatric technician career lesson ↗
QMHPQualified Mental Health Professional — QMHPCase management • skills training • care coordination • crisis support+

QMHPs commonly support people through behavioral health rehabilitation, skills training, case management, crisis support, advocacy, documentation, and community resource navigation.

You may enjoy this if: You like coordinating services, teaching skills, solving practical problems, and documenting care.

Explore the QMHP career lesson ↗
BHTBehavioral Health TechnicianDirect support • observation • safety • communication • teamwork+

Behavioral Health Technicians provide direct support to people receiving mental health, substance-use, residential, crisis, or community-based services as part of a larger provider team.

You may enjoy this if: You want an entry point into behavioral health with direct client support and close teamwork.

Where can this path take me?

Explore degree and advanced professional pathways.

You may enter behavioral health through a support role and advance over time—or go directly into a college or graduate pathway. Requirements vary by profession and license.

Graduate / Licensure

Licensed Professional Counselor / Therapist

Clinical counseling, assessment, treatment planning, psychotherapy, and specialty practice.

Bachelor's / Master's / Licensure

Social Worker

Direct services, case management, advocacy, community practice, clinical care, and systems-level work.

Psychologist

Assessment, diagnosis, intervention, research, consultation, and specialty psychological services.

Texas psychology licensing ↗
Nursing + Graduate Specialty

Psychiatric-Mental Health Nurse Practitioner

Advanced nursing practice focused on mental health assessment, diagnosis, treatment, and medication management within scope of practice.

Psychiatrist

Physician pathway specializing in the diagnosis and medical treatment of mental and behavioral health conditions.
